01 / Independent proposal

A 90-day club marketing strategy.

How a League of Ireland club could turn occasional matchgoers into repeat supporters without pretending it has a Premier League marketing budget.

Problem

Limited resources and an audience that may engage online without regularly attending.

Audience

Local families, students, grassroots players, lapsed supporters and new residents.

Approach

Three phases combining research, targeted content, local partnerships and ticket messaging.

Days 1–30 / Listen & measure

Audit existing channels, establish a baseline for attendance and conversions, identify audience segments, collect supporter feedback and understand booking friction.

Days 31–60 / Test campaigns

Run small localised creative tests, produce matchday explainer content and collaborate with schools, universities and community clubs. Track links, traffic and ticket enquiries by channel.

Days 61–90 / Refine & report

Keep what works, drop what doesn't and report against agreed baseline metrics. Assess ticket conversions, returning attendees, email sign-ups and campaign costs rather than follower count alone.

Proposed measures: match attendance, first-time and repeat ticket purchases, email acquisition, campaign click-through, cost per acquired supporter and supporter satisfaction. Targets must be agreed after baseline review.

02 / Independent communications proposal

Beyond the Matchday.

A football club has stories worth telling even when the first team hasn't kicked a ball.

Objective

Deepen supporter relationships and build an identity that doesn't depend on results.

Editorial pillars

Academy, community, staff, supporters, volunteers and the people behind the operation.

Execution

Short interviews, recurring behind-the-scenes features, community spotlights, newsletter content and coordinated club announcements.

Example monthly rhythm

Week one: academy pathway feature. Week two: an unsung staff or volunteer profile. Week three: a partner/community story with a genuine local angle. Week four: a supporter story or behind-the-scenes club operations piece. Build each story for website, newsletter and social platforms rather than creating four disconnected versions.

Evaluation: meaningful engagement, newsletter retention, content completion, partner feedback and supporter sentiment. Any access to players, staff or children would require the club's editorial and safeguarding approval.

03 / Independent commercial proposal

Partnerships worth more than a logo.

A sponsor should get more than a photograph beside an advertising board, and supporters should get more than another sales pitch.

Partner identification

Prioritise local businesses whose audience, geography and values genuinely align with the club.

Activation

Community coaching days, matchday experiences, local business stories and useful partner-backed content.

Commercial value

Create sponsorship tiers that combine visibility, hospitality, content access and measurable supporter engagement.

Sample partner proposition

A regional employer backs an academy storytelling series and two community events. The club provides a clear package of branded editorial placements, matchday mentions and agreed hospitality access. A report after each activation includes impressions, attendance, engagement and partner feedback, without presenting media reach as a guaranteed return on investment.

Evaluation: partner satisfaction, sponsor retention, event attendance, attributable leads where consent allows, activation costs and renewals. Actual inventory and pricing would depend on club approval and the market.
